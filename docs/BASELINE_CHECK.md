# BASELINE_CHECK.md: baselines against their original papers

Generated by `scripts/check_baselines.py`; our values are in
`results/baseline_check/original_settings.json`. Published values were read from
the PDFs of the original papers on 2026-11-02 (Table 5/6 of the GWO paper, the
results table of the WOA paper, Table 1 of Karaboga & Basturk 2007).

## Settings: D=30, 30 agents, 500 iterations, 30 runs (GWO, WOA, PSO papers)

Function ids of the GWO/WOA papers: F1 Sphere, F9 Rastrigin, F10 Ackley, F11 Griewank.

| Function | GWO paper | GWO here | WOA paper | WOA here | PSO (GWO paper) | PSO here |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sphere | 6.59e-28 | 1.03e-30 | 1.41e-30 | 1.41e-71 | 1.36e-4 | 5.88e-6 |
| Rastrigin | 0.311 | 9.01 | 0 | 0 | 46.7 | 51.4 |
| Ackley | 1.06e-13 | 3.51e-14 | 7.40 | 3.29e-15 | 0.276 | 1.39 |
| Griewank | 4.49e-3 | 5.61e-3 | 2.89e-4 | 7.38e-3 | 9.22e-3 | 0.0298 |

Reading:
- **GWO** agrees in order of magnitude on Sphere, Ackley and Griewank. Rastrigin is
  worse than published (9.01 vs 0.311); the published mean is close to zero and the
  distribution is heavy-tailed (published std 47.4 in the same table).
- **WOA** matches on Rastrigin. It is much stronger on Sphere and Ackley than the 2016
  table. We follow the authors' reference code (scalar A, C, l; per-dimension random
  leader), and do not claim to reproduce those two published means.
- **PSO** agrees in order of magnitude. Parameters differ: we use w=0.729, c1=c2=1.494,
  v_max=0.2(ub-lb); the comparison in the GWO paper uses other settings.

Agreement is good on Griewank, Rastrigin and Schwefel with limit=SN*D, not on
Rosenbrock and Ackley. The paper may use different initialisation ranges or
function variants for these two: our runs do not reproduce them and we make no claim of
equivalence there. The former limit=30 is clearly a handicap (Schwefel 690 vs 3.85e-4).

## Deviations found and fixed (all baselines re-run)

| Baseline | Defect | Evidence | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| GWO | Hierarchy updated inside the particle loop; dead loop drew 6 unused random vectors per wolf | Sphere 2.8e-79 vs published 6.6e-28 | Update once per iteration; dead loop removed (Sphere 1.0e-30) |
| WOA | `norm(A) < 1` on a D-dimensional vector; gbest updated inside the loop | Rastrigin 93.9 vs published 0 | Scalar A/C/l and per-dimension random leader, as in the reference code (Rastrigin 0) |
| ACO_R | sigma = xi*\|worst-best\| shared by all ants | Sphere mean 734 at the main budget | sigma_l = xi*sum_e \|s_e - s_l\|/(k-1), Socha & Dorigo 2008 (Sphere 5.9e-22) |
| PSO | No velocity clamp | Sphere 333 from one run at 10,000 | v_max = 0.2(ub-lb) (Sphere 1.6e-14) |
| ABC | limit=30, not the usual SN*D | Table above | limit = SN*D |

GWO and WOA keep their reference-code synchronous update. PSO, QPSO and the BioQPSO
family update gbest immediately, as before. This difference is part of the algorithms
as published and implemented, not a fairness adjustment.

The L-SHADE check against published CEC2017 values is done on the E3s results (see
`docs/FINAL_FIXES.md`). Note that opfunu F3 is official F4.

2016 (10.1016/j.advengsoft.2016.01.008), Karaboga & Basturk 2007
